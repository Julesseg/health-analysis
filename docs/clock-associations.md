# Clock-time associations

Use a daily clock field as an input, an adjustment input, or an output in Explore. A clock question uses the complete preceding day and can use full or shorter history. The available controls and model accept adjustments from supported daily fields. Clock interactions and multi-day clock summaries are unavailable. The app can suggest imported sleep onset and wake time as outputs when it has readable episodes.

## Days and provenance

- Manual times keep the Gregorian tracking date and wall-clock minute from the daily log. The saved analysis time zone identifies the dates to pair. It never converts a manual time to an instant or changes its clock reading. For example, a logged 02:30 on a skipped or repeated DST hour remains 02:30. An output from the daily log uses completed analysis dates, with its inputs ending before the output date begins.
- Imported sleep keeps the longest completed episode per selected source and recorded local wake date. Onset and wake use the episode's absolute start and end respectively. Onset uses the first asleep segment's time-zone evidence. Wake uses the episode's latest available asleep-segment time-zone evidence. Each falls back to the time zone retained at import when the relevant evidence is missing. These are best available local clocks, with possible error when recordings lack time-zone metadata or a source provides only one zone for a trip. Both endpoints retain absolute event provenance. The clock value uses the local hour and minute, so repeated minutes can share a clock value while the underlying instants remain distinct.
- For sleep timing, the input window ends before the earliest of the recorded wake-date label, the onset date in the analysis time zone, and the onset date in its event time zone. The calendar labels are interpreted in the saved analysis time zone. This conservative rule excludes behavior logged on the local onset day during travel. Sleep duration retains its existing pairing.
- Fitting includes defaults and weighting is equal across paired dates. Inspection retains exact and approximate precision on explicit manual times and the effective date for assumed defaults. Imported times retain source and zone evidence and are displayed to the minute. Reported uncertainty is conditional on the supplied times and does not quantify recall error or time-zone error.

## Clock interval and effect

For each clock series, remove the largest gap on the 24-hour circle among the paired clock values. The remaining interval must be shorter than 12 hours. Within that interval, unwrap values into minutes forward from the first paired clock value in the interval. Thus an interval from 23:30 to 00:30 has offsets of 0 and 60 minutes at its endpoints. The result shows those endpoints and describes earlier and later within that interval. No slope is estimated for values spread across half a day or more. Recomputing from more history can widen the interval or make an estimate unavailable.

Clock inputs are expressed per hour later. Each clock input needs recorded paired readings spanning at least an hour. A clock output reports a signed difference in wall-clock minutes. For example, 30 minutes per hour later means the model associates a one-hour later input with a 30-minute later output, with other modeled inputs held fixed. Use the linear model for clock and continuous outputs, the existing log-mean model for count outputs, and the existing in-range probability contrasts for yes/no outputs, and the rating threshold model for rating outputs. The clock interval is a local linearization with no inference outside its range.

Each clock field needs at least 12 paired explicit readings with at least 30 minutes between the recorded quartiles. The existing joint minimum of 42 days or 12 per fitted parameter, whichever is higher, also applies. Existing variation, collinearity and fit checks still apply. The nominal 95% interval reuses [calendar-day dependence adjustments](adjusted-numeric-associations.md). A usable result earns low confidence. The validation below does not calibrate stronger grades, support an all-day circular model, or estimate the effect of measurement error.

## Validation

- Application tests recover 30 minutes of bedtime difference per hour later from 23:30 to 00:30 and 10 minutes per additional coffee in the same fit. They reopen the log and saved results, preserving clock intervals and recorded precision.
- Hand-checkable sleep fixtures separate event instants, output wake labels, and the preceding behavior dates. They include Paris's spring shift, New York's second repeated 01:30, and a wake date that differs between Tokyo and UTC. The day's longest episode wins over a nap.
- Forty-eight seeded 240-day histories alternate a null effect and a known 24-minute effect across midnight, with persistent day-to-day noise. The test requires intervals to include the true effect in at least 38 trials, absolute mean bias below two minutes, and mean interval width below 24 minutes. A separate case verifies the yes/no output retains its probability scale after a clock input is unwrapped.
- A native test fixture drives clock selection, the effect and interval, Save, and paired precision evidence. The fixture uses synthetic daily log entries.

The clock transformation operates on a copy of the paired numeric values used by the model. Recorded values, sources, and origins remain available to the evidence views. Saved manual series keep their variable IDs. Sleep timing uses a separate output identity with the existing preferred-source behavior. Clock-specific metadata is optional in saved results. Automatic discovery continues to use its existing candidate family.
